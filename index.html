Drawing a laser-cut clock in Adobe Illustrator

This worked example builds a simple standing clock: a round face, a centre hole for the clock movement, engraved hour markers, a base and a slot-together stand. Use the same steps, then change the shapes to show your design era.

1

Set up the document

  1. Open Illustrator and choose File › New.
  2. Click the Print tab. Change Units to Millimeters.
  3. Set Width 300 mm and Height 300 mm. This is the size of your material sheet, so everything must fit inside it.
  4. Click Create. Save straight away as Surname_Clock.ai.

Save often with ⌘ Cmd + S. Made a mistake? Undo with ⌘ Cmd + Z.

2

Draw the clock face

Ellipse ToolShortcut L · hides under Rectangle
  1. Press L for the Ellipse Tool. Or click and hold the Rectangle Tool in the toolbar to find it.
  2. Click once on the artboard (don't drag). A box opens.
  3. Type Width 200 mm and Height 200 mm, then click OK.
  4. Set the colours: Fill = None and Stroke = red (C0 M100 Y100 K0). Press X to switch between the fill and stroke boxes, and / to set the front box to None.
  5. Open Window › Stroke and type 0.1 pt in Weight. Type the "pt" so Illustrator doesn't use mm.

A 0.1 pt line is very thin on screen. That's correct. Zoom in if you need to see it.

3

Cut the centre hole and line it up

Ellipse ToolL
Selection ToolV
  1. Click once with the Ellipse Tool and make a circle 8 × 8 mm. Measure your clock movement's shaft to check; most are 8 mm.
  2. Give it the same red 0.1 pt stroke and no fill. It is a cut.
  3. Press V for the Selection Tool and drag a box over both circles. (Or click one, then hold ⇧ Shift and click the other.)
  4. Click the big circle once more so it gets a thick outline. This makes it the key object that stays still.
  5. In Window › Align, click Horizontal Align Center and Vertical Align Center.

4

Add the hour markers

Rectangle ToolM
Rotate ToolR
  1. Click once with the Rectangle Tool and make a bar 3 × 12 mm.
  2. Give it a black fill (C0 M0 Y0 K100) and no stroke. This one engraves.
  3. Move it to the 12 o'clock spot, about 10 mm in from the edge. Line it up with the face using Horizontal Align Center (as in Step 3).
  4. Keep the bar selected. Choose the Rotate Tool, then hold ⌥ Option and click the exact centre of the hole. Smart Guides will say center.
  5. Type Angle 30° and click Copy (not OK). You now have the 1 o'clock marker.
  6. Press ⌘ Cmd + D ten times to repeat. All 12 markers appear.

Why 30°? A circle is 360°, and 360 ÷ 12 hours = 30°. For 60 minute ticks, use 6°.

5

Using numbers or letters instead

Type ToolT
  1. Click with the Type Tool and type one number. Pick a font that suits your design era.
  2. Make it black. Place each number by eye, or drag a marker from Step 4 to use as a guide, then delete it.
  3. When the numbers are finished, select them all and choose Type › Create Outlines (⇧ Shift + ⌘ Cmd + O). This turns the text into shapes.

Always create outlines. If the laser computer doesn't have your font, the numbers will change or disappear.

6

Add a base with Pathfinder

Rectangle ToolM
Pathfinder: UniteWindow › Pathfinder
Shape Builder⇧M · optional
  1. Draw a rectangle 120 × 50 mm and slide it so it overlaps the bottom of the face.
  2. Select the face circle and the rectangle only. Click one, hold ⇧ Shift, click the other. Leave the hole and markers out.
  3. In Pathfinder, click Unite. The two shapes join into one outline.
  4. Check the new outline is still red, 0.1 pt, no fill. Pathfinder sometimes copies the wrong colours.

This is where your design era comes in. Unite or Minus Front any shapes you like: Art Deco steps, a pagoda roof, a sunburst. The rule stays the same. The outer edge must be one red line.

7

Make the stand with matching slots

The face and stand slide together in a cross. Each piece has a slot as wide as the material is thick, cut halfway into the joint.

  1. Measure your material with callipers. This example uses 3 mm acrylic.
  2. Slot in the face: draw a rectangle 3 × 25 mm. Line it up with the centre of the base, touching the bottom edge. Select it and the outline, then click Pathfinder Minus Front.
  3. The stand: draw a rectangle 120 × 50 mm. Cut a 3 × 25 mm slot down from the top centre with Minus Front.
  4. Both pieces should be red 0.1 pt with no fill.

Two 25 mm slots add up to the full 50 mm height, so the pieces sit flush. Test cut the slot in scrap first; if it's loose, make it 2.8 mm.

8

Separate your parts by material and save

The laser cuts one sheet of material at a time, so every material needs its own 300 × 300 mm board. You can use the wood plus up to 3 acrylic colours.

  1. Open Window › Artboards and click the + button to add a new artboard. It copies the 300 × 300 mm size. Add one for each material you use.
  2. Move each part onto the board for its material. Cut with ⌘ Cmd + X, click the new board, then paste in the same spot with ⇧ Shift + ⌘ Cmd + V.
  3. Put the title at the top left of each board with the Type Tool, written exactly like this:
    • Wood DESIGN (Your NAME)
    • White Acrylic (Your NAME)
    • Yellow Acrylic (Your NAME)
    • Blue Acrylic (Your NAME)
    Use the real colour of your acrylic and your own name. Keep the title clear of your parts.
  4. Wood DESIGN has the clock face: red cut outline and hole, black engraving.
  5. Acrylic boards are cut only, so every line on them is red 0.1 pt.
  6. Delete any empty boards, save with ⌘ Cmd + S and take a screenshot of each board for your folio.

Maximum 3 acrylic colours. Parts in the same colour go on the same board.

Before you send it to the laser

  • Every cut line is CMYK 0, 100, 100, 0 at 0.1 pt with no fill.
  • Every engraving is CMYK 0, 0, 0, 100.
  • All text is outlined (Type › Create Outlines). No live text left.
  • No doubled lines. Two lines on top of each other cut twice and burn the edge.
  • The centre hole matches your clock movement (about 8 mm).
  • Slots are the same width as your material.
  • Engravings sit at least 5 mm in from any cut edge.
  • Everything fits on the sheet and inside the artboard.
  • Each material is on its own 300 × 300 mm board, titled "Material (Your NAME)".
  • No more than 3 acrylic colours, plus the wood.
  • File saved as .ai with your name, plus screenshots for your folio.
